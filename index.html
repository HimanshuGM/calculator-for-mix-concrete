Scope: IS 10262:2019 Section 2 — ordinary and standard grades, M10 to M60, for reinforced concrete (RCC), with durability requirements checked against IS 456:2000. Plain concrete (PCC) uses different durability limits and is not covered. Grades M65 and above (Section 3, high‑strength concrete) use different water-content, w/cm and aggregate tables and are also not covered by this tool.

SCM (fly ash / GGBS / silica fume) replacement percentages, admixture water-reduction percentages, and the pumpability adjustment are all trial inputs — this tool does not independently certify that a given value is code-compliant for your materials; establish them from the applicable material standard, project specification and trial mixes.

Cl. 5.1, Fig. 1, IS 456 Tbl. 5

The strength-based w/c ratio is to be obtained by reading the applicable strength–w/c curve in IS 10262:2019 Fig. 1 for the selected cement type and target mean strength (Curve 1: OPC 33 / expected 28‑day strength 33–<43 N/mm²; Curve 2: OPC 43 / 43–<53 N/mm²; Curve 3: OPC 53 / ≥53 N/mm²). 05

Cementitious material content

Cl. 5.4

Cement content follows from the water content and the free w/c ratio you selected in Step 3, checked against the IS 456 Table 5 durability minimum (20mm-aggregate basis). When fly ash / GGBS is used, Cl. 5.4.1 allows increasing the calculated cementitious content by 10% for the preliminary trial (as done in Annex B/C of the standard). The w/c you entered stays fixed as the design input; once SCM is added, the actual governing ratio becomes w/cm (water ÷ total cementitious mass) — identical to w/c when there's no SCM, lower than it once SCM is present.

IS 456 Table 5's minimum cement figures are given on a 20mm-aggregate basis; IS 10262 does not itself tabulate a fixed correction for other sizes. This field defaults to 0 (no adjustment). Some design offices apply their own convention here — enter your project's value, or leave at 0 and rely on the Table 5 minimum directly.

Cl. 5.5, Tbl. 5

The coarse-aggregate volume fraction depends on nominal max. size and fine-aggregate zone, corrected for the actual w/c ratio (±0.01 per ±0.05 change from 0.50), then optionally reduced up to 10% for pumped concrete (Cl. 5.5.2).

08

Site batch correction

Illustrated in Annex A-11

Design-stage quantities (Step 7) assume saturated-surface-dry (SSD) aggregate. Moisture correction converts those SSD design quantities into actual as-batched (wet) quantities, and adjusts the water added at the mixer for the moisture and absorption of the aggregate — via an oven-dry basis, consistent with Annex B/A-11 of the standard. Enter the total moisture content as measured on site (e.g. by a moisture meter or oven-dry test); absorption comes from laboratory data for the aggregate source.

Admixture water is neglected unless you enter the admixture's dry solids content in Step 4. If entered, the water carried by the admixture, admixture mass × (1 − solids/100), is deducted from the mixer water below. If left blank, the admixture is assumed 100% solid (or its water is accounted for on site) and nothing is deducted.

Design Checks

Verification & Limitations

  • Mix proportions are calculated using the implemented IS 10262:2019 methodology and the selected design inputs.
  • Durability requirements are checked against the implemented IS 456:2000 limits.
  • The adopted w/c ratio should be confirmed using the applicable IS 10262 guidance / Fig. 1 and trial data.
  • Manufactured/crushed sand does not receive an invented universal automatic correction.
  • Moisture correction depends on the entered aggregate moisture and absorption values.
  • Trial mixes and laboratory validation are required before field implementation.
  • Final engineering approval remains the responsibility of the qualified designer/engineer.
